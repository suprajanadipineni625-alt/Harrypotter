# The story

**This replaces docs/CONTENT.md.** That document braided abstract moments across
films and rendered them as light and particles. The reference direction is
different in kind: an *illustrated narrative* — painted scenes, display type,
prose, parallax — telling one continuous story with **recognisable moments**.

Reference direction: flat painterly illustration, deep teal-and-blue night
palette, warm lit windows, a castle silhouette against cloud, a paragraph of
narrative prose per scene, scroll-driven parallax between layers.

---

## The spine: the Horcrux hunt

The thread that makes eight parts one story rather than eight summaries.

Seven Horcruxes are made, then destroyed one at a time across the series. The
first is destroyed in Part 2 and the last in Part 8. **The site counts them.** A
persistent element — seven marks that go out one by one as you scroll — sits
with you the whole way, so every part is visibly part of one hunt rather than a
self-contained chapter.

| # | Horcrux | Destroyed in | By |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The diary | Part 2 | Harry, basilisk fang |
| 2 | The ring | Part 6 | Dumbledore |
| 3 | The locket | Part 7 | Ron, sword of Gryffindor |
| 4 | The cup | Part 8 | Hermione, basilisk fang |
| 5 | The diadem | Part 8 | Fiendfyre, Room of Requirement |
| 6 | Nagini | Part 8 | Neville, sword of Gryffindor |
| 7 | Harry | Part 8 | Voldemort, in the forest |

That last row is the payoff. The counter you have been watching for seven parts
resolves on the one Horcrux nobody knew was there.

## The eight parts

Each part is a small number of scenes. A scene = one illustrated composition,
one paragraph of prose, one scroll beat.

### Part 1 — The Philosopher's Stone
*Wonder. Everything is new.*

1. **The letters.** Envelopes pouring through the fireplace, the letterbox, the
   windows — filling the room. The opening image, and it moves on the first
   frame.
2. **Platform nine and three quarters.** The barrier, the scarlet engine, steam.
3. **The Sorting.** The Great Hall, floating candles, the hat, four tables in
   four colours. *This is where the house palette is established — it pays off
   in every later part.*
4. **The chess game.** The giant board, Ron's sacrifice, the knight's last move.
5. **The Mirror.** The stone, and the first time Voldemort is close.

### Part 2 — The Chamber of Secrets
*The first Horcrux, though nobody knows it yet.*

1. **The flying car** over the countryside.
2. **The writing on the wall**, wet and red, in a torchlit corridor.
3. **The diary** — pages absorbing ink, writing back.
4. **The Chamber.** Stone serpents, green water, the basilisk in shadow.
5. **The fang through the diary.** Ink pours out like blood. **Horcrux 1 of 7.**

### Part 3 — The Prisoner of Azkaban
*The first time light is something you cast.*

1. **Dementors on the train.** Frost creeping across the glass.
2. **The Marauder's Map** — ink footprints moving across parchment.
3. **Buckbeak over the lake.**
4. **The Patronus.** Silver light driving the dark back. **The interactive beat.**
5. **The Time-Turner.** The same night, seen twice.

### Part 4 — The Goblet of Fire
*It stops being school.*

1. **The Goblet**, blue flame, a name it should not have produced.
2. **The dragon** over the roofs of the castle.
3. **The lake** — down into cold black water.
4. **The maze**, hedges closing.
5. **The graveyard.** Voldemort returns. The palette goes cold and stays cold.

### Part 5 — The Order of the Phoenix
*Institutional dread — the enemy is inside.*

1. **The quill.** Words cut into the back of a hand. Pink, gilt, unbearable.
2. **Dumbledore's Army** in the Room of Requirement.
3. **The Hall of Prophecy** — shelves receding into dark.
4. **The shatter.** Thousands of orbs falling.
5. **The veil.** Sirius, and the first real loss.

### Part 6 — The Half-Blood Prince
*The hunt begins in earnest.*

1. **The Prince's book**, annotated in a stranger's hand.
2. **The memory** — Slughorn's, silver, unwinding.
3. **The cave.** Black water, green fire, hands rising. The locket.
4. **The tower.** Green light. Dumbledore falls.
5. **The ring**, already destroyed — Dumbledore's blackened hand explained.
   **Horcrux 2 of 7.**

### Part 7 — The Deathly Hallows, Part One
*Nowhere to go home to.*

1. **The empty house.** Grimmauld Place, dust, a burnt-out family tapestry.
2. **On the run** — a tent in a wilderness that changes every night.
3. **The locket**, worn by each of them, poisoning whoever carries it.
4. **The frozen lake.** Silver light under ice. The sword.
5. **The sword through the locket.** **Horcrux 3 of 7.**
6. **The Three Brothers.** Ink-and-shadow silhouette — a total shift of medium,
   and the breath before the end.
7. **Dobby.** A grave dug by hand above a bright, terrible beach.

### Part 8 — The Deathly Hallows, Part Two
*Everything at once, and then everything gone.*

1. **Gringotts.** The vault, the cup, the dragon breaking out through the roof.
   **Horcrux 4 of 7.**
2. **The Room of Requirement.** Fiendfyre takes the diadem. **5 of 7.**
3. **The shield.** A dome of light rising over the whole castle.
4. **The battle.** The castle breaking. Statues walking. *Everything is gone* —
   the Great Hall from Part 1 in ruins, the chess board from Part 1 echoed in
   the courtyard.
5. **Snape's memories.** Silver, and the whole story rewritten in one minute.
6. **The forest.** Walking to his own death with the dead beside him.
   **Horcrux 7 of 7 — himself.**
7. **King's Cross.** White. Nothing but white.
8. **The last move.** Neville, the sword, Nagini. **6 of 7 — out of order,
   which is the point.** Then the duel, and the wand that was never his.
9. **Dawn.** The castle in first light. The counter reads seven of seven.

## What connects the parts

1. **The Horcrux counter.** Seven marks, always present, going out one at a time.
2. **The house palette.** Established at the Sorting in Part 1; every later part
   is graded against those four colours.
3. **Rhymes.** The Great Hall whole in Part 1 and ruined in Part 8. The chess
   board and the courtyard. The letters arriving and the letters burning.
4. **One continuous colour journey.** Warm gold → cold → dark → dawn, the arc
   the films themselves already shoot.

## The hard constraint, restated

The reference uses the film's own wordmark and typeface. **We cannot.** Nor
film stills, nor official crests.

What we CAN do is original illustration of these scenes, which is exactly what
the reference site is — painted artwork, not film frames. Fan artwork of a
scene is a different thing from the studio's asset for that scene. Everything
in BUILD-CONSTRAINTS §5 still applies: non-commercial, original artwork, the
name out of the domain, and a display face that is emphatically not the film's.
